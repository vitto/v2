# Questions before designing

The context a design must fit, for UX, UI and visual design, beyond what the topics of the interview already ask (the goal, the people and their paths, their world and words, the entities and the views). Thirteen parts, in the order they are asked; each says when it can be skipped as a whole. Each question says what its answer changes.

Written in English: ask them in the language of the interview, in natural words, never word by word. A question the conversation, the notes or the project already answer is proposed to confirm, not asked. Ask a few at a time, and follow what the person says, as the interview does.

## 1. Goal and success

Why it is made and for whom, the problem it removes, how success is measured, what ships first. **Skip when**: never.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| What is the riskiest assumption now: that people want this, that it can be built, or that it is usable? | UX | What the first prototype must prove |
| What exactly do we want to learn from this release, and which data must we capture? | UX | Tracking and events designed into screens |
| What strategic themes and quarterly outcome goals does leadership have that this work must serve? | UX | Priorities aligned to business roadmap |
| Which beliefs about users, market or technology is this brief built on that nobody has checked yet? | UX | What to validate before detailed design |
| Which change in what people do will tell us the work succeeded, and how is it measured? | UX | Success criteria and analytics to plan |
| What outcome do stakeholders expect, rather than which documents or mock-ups they asked for? | UX | Deliverables chosen to serve the outcome |
| Why is this being built, for whom, and what would make those who asked for it call it a success? | UX | Scope, priorities and what the design optimises for |
| Which exact area of the product and which measured number does 'better experience' stand for here? | UX | Concrete targets instead of vague adjectives |
| What are the current baselines for the key behaviour metrics, and what end goals are set? | UX | Benchmarks for judging the design |
| What is the smallest useful version that can ship, and which nice-to-haves can wait? | UX | Scope of the first design |

## 2. People and their world

Who they are, their groups, needs and feelings, how they work today, their words and mental models, the products they know. **Skip when**: the people are already known and written down (an object model exists).

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Who gets value from this: users, customers, the business, people affected indirectly, and do their interests clash? | UX | Priorities when audiences disagree |
| Which moments of the journey are the most intense, and how does it end? | UX | Where delight and care are concentrated |
| Which complexity in this domain cannot be removed, and who should carry it: the system or the user? | UX | What the system handles automatically |
| Who reads the interface text, what is their language level, and which jargon do they already use? | UX | Vocabulary and tone of copy |
| Do users have manuals or training today, or must guidance live inside the product? | UI | In-context help and onboarding design |
| Which platform conventions and competitor patterns do users already know that this product should match? | UI | Component choices and familiar behaviour |

## 3. What is already known

Feedback, support, searches, usage data, known problems, who can be heard. **Skip when**: a new product with no users yet.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| What do people search for on the site and fail to find? | UX | Navigation labels, content gaps, search design |
| What do usage logs and funnels show about where people drop off today? | UX | Which steps get redesigned first |
| Which known UX problems and unfinished journeys already exist across teams, and who owns them? | UX | Debt to fix versus new design |
| Which commissioners, colleagues, users and readers can we listen to before and during the work? | UX | Research plan, feedback loops |

## 4. People around the project

Who commissions and decides, an outside client or referents and teams of your own company you are not part of, how involved they are and who must be kept informed, who builds it and how they work with design, the reach of the team, compliance, who is affected. **Skip when**: nobody but you decides, reviews, builds it or is affected by it (a personal project).

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Who must be kept informed along the way, and how do they want to hear about risks and progress? | UX | Reporting rhythm and format, fewer late surprises |
| Who decides on this, an outside client or people of your own company, how involved will they be, and who owns priorities? | UX | Review rhythm, who signs off, decision speed |
| Which roles from other teams, such as product, development, QA or marketing, will work with you, as peers or by hand-off? | UX | Shared reviews or hand-offs, how detailed deliverables are |
| Does compliance or the organisation require specific documents, and when must they be delivered? | UX | Which deliverables to plan and when |
| Which departments and customers are affected by this change, and must they be warned or given an opt-out? | UX | Rollout, messaging and transition options |
| Who, inside or outside your company, owns the business problem and goals, and can they join the early sessions? | UX | Who shapes the framing, whose goals the design serves |
| How much authority does this team have over the problem: a whole business unit or one feature? | UX | Size of the problem the design may touch |
| What design principles or values does the team already hold, and what should this project follow? | UX | Shared criteria for design decisions |
| Who will build it: an internal team, another department or an outside vendor? | UI | Handoff format and prototyping depth |

## 5. What already exists

Design system, current look, earlier layouts and documents, the application it lives in, references admired. **Skip when**: a new product with nothing before it.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Which documents does the organisation already produce, and who is bound to follow a design manual? | UX | Manual scope, grid devised across documents |
| Which products do the people who commissioned it and the users admire, and what exactly do they like? | UX | Design references and direction |
| Does the new feature live inside an existing application, and which analytics and integrations already exist there? | UI | Constraints on structure and measurement |
| Is there an existing design system or component library, who owns it, and what code implements it? | UI | Reuse of components, tokens and constraints |
| Is there an earlier layout or grid, and does it truly suit this job? | Visual | Reuse versus a grid made for this job |
| What is the product's current look, feel and brand that a new feature must match? | Visual | Visual style inherited for the new piece |

## 6. Where and how it is seen or used

Devices and input, the room on screen, place, light, distance and scale, accessibility needs. **Skip when**: never.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Where will people see it: in what place and light, from how far, and next to what? | UX | Type size, contrast, placement |
| Which users have vision, motor, cognitive or situational limits that the design must account for, and what standard is required? | UX | Sets accessibility floor, contrast, target sizes |
| On which devices, with which input methods and in which postures will people use this? | UI | Target sizes, placement and reach |
| Where will this live: full-width page, narrow panel, embed or modal, and how wide can it be? | UI | Container width and layout |
| Which devices, outputs and future uses must this content be reformatted for? | UI | Style-sheet separation, content structure |
| At what physical scale will the type appear: small format, poster, or building signage? | Visual | Type size, drama, pacing of letters |

## 7. Content and reading

What content and how much, its kinds and structure, how it is read and moved through, languages. **Skip when**: a tool with little text, mostly forms and data.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| What one message must people take away, even if they read nothing else? | UX | What leads, what can be cut |
| How will people move through this material: in order, or jumping, searching and returning? | UX | Navigation devices, contents, index |
| Is the material one narrative or many standalone chunks that people may need assembled on demand? | UX | Linear versus modular structure |
| Will users arrive impatient and searching, or settled and ready to read? | UX | Scanning support, density, hierarchy |
| Do readers need to compare and connect pieces of information side by side? | UX | Dense single surface versus separate pages |
| How much text is truly needed, and can any of it be cut? | UX | Copy length, space use |
| Is the interface dense, like a dashboard, or content-led, like reading or marketing? | UI | Base size, density of type and spacing |
| How long is the typical text block, and is it read at length or only scanned? | UI | Line length, size and line height |
| In which locales, languages and number formats will the product be used, including right-to-left? | UI | Alignment, number formats, text expansion room |
| What must live in the margins: page numbers, running heads, notes, navigation? | UI | Margin size and contents |
| For what use will the running text be read, and where? | Visual | Body type size |
| How much text and how many pictures will each page carry, and of what kinds? | Visual | Type area, columns, margins |
| Will there be footnotes, captions, marginal notes, and how many large or small pictures? | Visual | Column count, side column, type area |
| Which scripts and languages must appear, and where do translations sit? | Visual | Grid fit, fixed place for translations |
| Does the content have a complex hierarchy or mix text with illustrations? | Visual | Column count, zones, spanning |

## 8. Tasks and flows

The main tasks and actions, what comes first, long tasks, errors, destructive actions, waits, empty states, help. **Skip when**: a piece that is only read (a poster, an article).

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Which parts of the product are genuinely unique, and which tasks can safely follow patterns users already know? | UX | Where to innovate versus follow conventions |
| Which few tasks and features carry most of the real use, and which cases are rare? | UX | Prioritises design effort and screen emphasis |
| How many questions does the process really need, how long does it take, and what must people have at hand? | UX | Form steps, progress and upfront guidance |
| Which errors can really happen, what causes each, and how can users recover from them? | UX | Content and placement of error messages |
| Which single action is the most important in each view, according to the business and the user? | UX | Primary action and button hierarchy |
| How costly is each destructive action for users or the business, and can it be undone? | UX | Confirmation friction or undo |
| Which views begin empty because users create the content, and what should a first-time person do there? | UX | Empty state design and call to action |
| What waits will this product have, such as loading, processing or server calls, and how long are they? | UI | Loading states and progress feedback |
| Which tasks are long or multi-step, and where do people tend to abandon them? | UI | Progress indicators and step structure |
| How long do users expect this task to take, and which information can be filled in for them? | UI | Form length, autofill, task duration |

## 9. Data, forms and numbers

What is asked of people, choices, real values, tables and charts. **Skip when**: people neither enter nor read structured data.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| What is the minimum data the product truly needs from people, and why does each field matter? | UX | Which form fields exist at all |
| What are the longest, shortest and strangest real values, such as names and addresses, that content will take? | UI | Component flexibility and truncation rules |
| How many options does each choice offer, and do users know what they want to find? | UI | Radio buttons, dropdown or type-ahead |
| Which data fields are shown, and which are self-explanatory in format versus needing a label? | UI | Whether data needs labels |
| Which tables and numeric data appear, with what precision, units and currency? | UI | Number alignment, column formats, figure style |
| Which charts, status changes or comparisons exist, and do colour-blind users rely on them? | UI | Colour plus icon or contrast cues |
| What is the structure of the underlying data, and what do people look for in it? | UI | Hierarchy mirroring data and site structure |

## 10. Brand, personality and tone

The character the look carries, tone of voice, colours and typefaces owned, mood, brand elements and what they mean. **Skip when**: the visual identity is given and fixed (then only What already exists).

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| What do people expect from this look and brand, and how does that compare with the planned experience? | Visual | Visual direction adjusted to expectations |
| Should the product feel safe and universal, or dramatic and luxurious, and does it live in light or dark settings? | Visual | Light or dark ground |
| What mood should neutrals carry: cool, warm, neutral, and does the brand have a reference tone? | Visual | Tint of greys, blacks and whites |
| Does the brand already own a colour, and does it clash with colours that carry system meaning? | Visual | Brand colour and action colour |
| Does the brand already use typefaces, and are licences and web-font usage allowed? | Visual | Typeface choice and licensing limits |
| Does the brand need a distinct voice in headings, such as classic, playful, handmade or luxurious? | Visual | Whether a second typeface is added |
| What personality should the product convey: serious, playful, elegant, neutral, or something else? | Visual | Typeface, colour and corner radius choices |
| Which semantic states and accent colours does the product need, such as warning, success, highlight or category? | Visual | Size of the palette beyond brand colour |
| Does the brand already use patterns, illustrations or shapes, and what does each of them mean? | Visual | Which brand elements appear, and only with meaning |
| Does the brand have a story of making or origin that a typeface's construction could echo? | Visual | Display face, expressive treatment |
| What audience, content and connotations must the typeface carry, and which families already suit the body text? | Visual | Typeface choice, body-text family |
| Should the tone be formal or informal, and how much may the layout show it? | Visual | Alignment choice, centred versus seamed |

## 11. Images

Who supplies photos and illustrations, their style and rights, images people upload, icon and logo sizes. **Skip when**: there are no pictures.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Which images will users upload, in what sizes, ratios and quality, and may they clash with the background? | UI | Image containers, cropping and edge treatment |
| What photographic, illustration and statistics styles does the company already use? | Visual | Image style rules, colour per content type |
| Will photos be decorative or product shots, and what colour temperature do the brand and its photography share? | Visual | Photo treatment and palette temperature |
| Who supplies photography and illustration, and what quality, style and rights exist for it? | Visual | Image style, sourcing and placeholders |
| Which sizes and contexts must icons, logos and screenshots appear in, such as favicon, card or hero? | Visual | Asset versions drawn per size |

## 12. Across pieces and media

The media now and later, formats, what stays constant across pieces, recurring elements, the breadth of the type system, print format and binding. **Skip when**: a single screen or a single piece.

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Which paper sizes or formats must the piece use, and are they standard? | Visual | Page format, production cost |
| Which media does the company use now and later, and what must stay distinctive for years? | Visual | Identity scope, grid, type, colour together |
| Which recurring text elements exist, and who must keep them fixed across documents? | Visual | Fixed sizes, positions, leading rules |
| How many weights, widths and voices must one typographic system cover across its pieces? | Visual | Family size, weights, widths |
| In which contexts must the logotype appear, and how much may the identity change between them? | Visual | Logotype versions, identity flexibility |
| What must stay constant across all the pages or pieces, and what may vary? | Visual | Fixed constants versus variable layout parts |
| Will it be read as single pages or spreads, and how will it be bound? | Visual | Margins, spread design |

## 13. How text is written and reviewed

Who edits, in what form text arrives, who collects corrections. **Skip when**: no editorial flow (labels and copy written by the designer).

| Question | Area | What the answer changes |
|----------|------|-------------------------|
| Who is the editor, separate from author and designer, and in which phases do they work? | UX | Review schedule, role ownership |
| In what form will the text arrive from the author, and how clean is it? | UX | Content intake, formatting effort |
| Who is the single person collecting all reviewers' corrections into one master proof? | UX | Review flow, correction ownership |
